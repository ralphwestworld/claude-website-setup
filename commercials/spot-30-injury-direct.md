# Buckeye Law Group — 30s "What's Your Case Really Worth"

Original creative for an injury-law spot in the proven genre pattern (Morgan
& Morgan / Jim Adler / Ken Nugent / Cellino & Barnes). No copy lifted from
any existing spot — the *patterns* are common to the category, the *words*
are ours.

## The brief, restated

A real injury commercial — money-forward, urgent, adversary frame against
the insurance company. CTA = "call us and find out what your case is
really worth." Targeted via geofence around hospitals, urgent cares, and
collision centers. Greg Gudin on camera; voice-clone for VO and end card.

## The pattern we're copying (deliberately)

| Genre tactic                      | Where it shows up                  | Our version                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Adversary hook in first 3 seconds | Adler "hammer," Morgan "size matters" | "The insurance company already decided what your accident is worth. They're wrong." |
| Founder on camera, named          | John Morgan, Jim Adler personally  | Greg Gudin, named twice                           |
| Big dollar credibility number     | Morgan: "billions recovered"       | "Over a billion dollars recovered for Ohioans"    |
| Educational hook                  | Morgan: "rights you didn't know"   | "Find out what your case is really worth"         |
| Single CTA, repeated, on-screen   | Cellino & Barnes 800-888-8888      | 1‑800‑411‑PAIN held on end card 6+ seconds        |
| "Free / no fee unless we win"     | Genre standard                     | End card supers it                                |

## Spec

- **Length:** :30 master, with :15 and :06 cutdowns at the bottom.
- **Voice:** Greg Gudin. Decisive, slightly clipped — closer to a closing
  argument than a friendly chat. Not yelling. Not folksy.
- **Aspect:** 16×9 master, 9×16 mobile vertical (the geofence inventory),
  1×1 in-feed.
- **Tonal rule:** the *implication* of an accident, not the depiction. No
  blood, no impact SFX, no on-screen kids. Better legally and it tests
  better.
- **Single CTA, on screen for the final ~7 seconds.**

## Script — :30 master

Total VO budget ~80 words.

```
[0:00 – 0:04]   GREG (on camera, MCU, hard-edge lighting, plain background)
                "The insurance company already decided what your accident
                 is worth. They're wrong."

[0:04 – 0:11]   GREG (VO over B‑roll: lowball check, ER paperwork, missed
                paystubs, brace on a kitchen table)
                "Their first offer won't cover your medical bills. Your
                 lost wages. Your pain. Or what comes next."

[0:11 – 0:14]   GREG (on camera, walks into firm conference room — team
                visible behind him, files on the table)
                "I'm Greg Gudin, Buckeye Law Group."

[0:14 – 0:20]   GREG (VO, lower-third super: "$1,000,000,000+ Recovered
                for Injured Ohioans" + small disclaimer)
                "We've recovered over a billion dollars for injured
                 Ohioans — because we know what your case is really worth.
                 And they do too."

[0:20 – 0:24]   GREG (on camera, slight push-in, direct)
                "Don't sign anything. Don't settle. Call us first."

[0:24 – 0:30]   END CARD (held)
                Super: 1‑800‑411‑PAIN
                Sub: "Find out what your case is really worth."
                Sub: "No fee unless we win."
                VO tag: "1‑800‑411‑PAIN. Buckeye Law Group."
```

### Why each line is doing work

- **0:00–0:04 — adversary hook.** "They're wrong" lets the viewer feel
  vindicated in 4 seconds. The viewer has already been talked down to by
  an adjuster today, and this spot agrees with them.
- **0:04–0:11 — pain inventory.** Bills, wages, pain, future. Four-beat
  rhythm. The "future" line is the one Morgan & Morgan keeps winning on —
  it's what the lowball offer is hiding.
- **0:11–0:14 — name and brand.** Founder on camera, named, with team
  visibly behind him. This is the "you have a firm, not a guy" beat —
  Buckeye's actual structural advantage over a solo shop.
- **0:14–0:20 — credibility number.** Buckeye's real "$1B+ recovered"
  claim, supered with the bar-compliant disclaimer. "And they do too" is
  the sting — *the insurance company knows what it's worth and is hoping
  you don't ask*. That's the educational hook.
- **0:20–0:24 — three commands.** "Don't sign. Don't settle. Call." Imperative
  voice. One of the highest-converting closes in the genre.
- **0:24–0:30 — single CTA, held.** Phone number on screen for 6+ seconds,
  said twice. Repetition is the conversion.

### B‑roll we deliberately don't shoot

- Crash footage. Triggers platform throttling and undermines the calm-pro
  authority frame.
- Hospital interior with patients. Bar review hates it; programmatic
  platforms throttle it.
- Stock gavel-on-block. Lawyer-ad bingo.
- Fanned cash. Reads cheap; we have a real billion-dollar claim — show
  *files and verdicts*, not Monopoly money.

## On-screen disclaimers (Ohio Rules of Professional Conduct 7.x — verify with firm GC)

- Persistent: "ADVERTISING MATERIAL" small, lower-left or upper-right
  throughout.
- Under the $1B card (full duration of card): "Past results do not
  guarantee a similar outcome. Each case is unique. Total recoveries
  include verdicts, settlements, and gross figures before fees and costs."
- End card: principal office "127 Public Square, Cleveland, OH" small
  type. "Gregory E. Gudin, Esq., responsible attorney."

## What we deliberately did NOT do (per user direction + compliance)

- We don't claim an "average $200K" or any other average dollar figure.
  National average car-accident settlement in 2026 is ~$30K; serious
  injuries run $80K–$250K. A "$200K average" claim would be misleading
  and triggers Ohio Rule 7.1 ("false or misleading communication"). The
  CTA "find out what your case is **really** worth" delivers the same
  emotional payload — *they're underpaying you* — without the false claim.

## Variants worth shooting in the same session

Same wardrobe, same Greg, swap a handful of lines and shots. Each one is
targeted to a different geofence layer.

- **Body-shop / collision pull** (geofence: collision centers, tow yards,
  auto-glass). Open swap: "Your car's totaled. The adjuster's already
  decided what it's worth — and what *you're* worth. They're wrong."
- **Hospital / urgent-care pull** (geofence: hospital adjacent, urgent
  cares, PT clinics — NOT inside facilities; see compliance note in
  research doc). Open swap: "You just left the ER. The bills are
  starting. Their first call is coming."
- **Truck / commercial-vehicle pull** (geofence: highway corridors, truck
  stops, trauma-center catchments). Swap shot 2 to a long-haul rig on
  rainy I‑71. Add: "Trucking companies have teams of lawyers on speed
  dial. So should you."
- **Workers' comp pull** (geofence: union halls, industrial parks, OSHA
  area offices). Swap to job-site B‑roll; add the workers'-comp practice
  line.
- **Spanish-language read.** Same script, Greg or partner read. Cleveland
  / Columbus geofences with Spanish-dominant ZIPs.

## Open creative questions before lock

1. **End-card phone vs. URL.** Recommend phone-only on broadcast and OTT;
   add a vanity URL (e.g., buckeyepain.com) on mobile-vertical only —
   thumbs are already on the device there.
2. **Sting word: "really" vs. "actually."** Field-test "what your case is
   really worth" vs. "what your case is actually worth." A/B in geofence
   delivery for two weeks; pick the winner for the network buy.
3. **Open with adversary hook (current) vs. educational hook** ("Most
   people accept the first offer. Most people leave money on the
   table"). Shoot both opens in the same session — 10 minutes on set,
   gives us the data later.
4. **Real client cameo (3‑second cutaway) — yes or no.** Boosts
   testimonial credibility but adds a release form and a casting
   decision. Recommend leaving slot 8 (0:17–0:20) flexible: shoot both
   the adjuster-POV version AND a 3‑second client cutaway version,
   decide in the edit.
